## I. Light Propagation in Vacuum: The Lowest-Stress Steady State

In vacuum, the node spacing is on the order of $0.1 \sim 1$ m. When a traveling wave (light) propagates along the node network:

- Each node produces an extremely weak phase perturbation on the traveling wave;
- The perturbation amplitude is far below the threshold for activating connections;
- Light propagates smoothly step by step, without producing violent stress release.

**N2T mechanism**: the node network is in an extremely low-stress state; the traveling wave propagates along stress-free edges, producing only phase perturbations without triggering connection activation.

## II. Airglow: Continuous Radiation from Moderate Stress with Partially Activated Edges

When phase differences between a large number of nodes in the atmosphere persist, but the overall stress remains below the lightning breakdown threshold:

- Stress in some regions reaches an "intermediate threshold," and edges between a small number of nodes become partially activated;
- These edges are in a "semi-connected" state: energy continues to flow in, but is insufficient to form a complete discharge channel;
- The energy on the edges radiates outward in the form of low-frequency traveling waves (visible light)—manifesting as diffuse glow.

**N2T mechanism**: stress accumulation reaches the intermediate threshold $\theta_{\text{airglow}}$；the node network is in a "semi-connected" state, continuously radiating energy without violent release.

## III. Aurora: Continuous Radiation Excited by High-Energy Particles

The aurora is a continuous luminescence phenomenon produced when high-energy particles from the solar wind enter Earth's atmosphere and excite the high-altitude polar node network:

- The solar wind carries a large number of high-energy free nodes (particles) into Earth's atmosphere, moving along the phase gradient direction of Earth's magnetic field;
- These high-energy free nodes undergo phase coupling with the atmospheric node network at high polar altitudes, causing some atmospheric nodes to enter a "semi-connected" state;
- Energy is continuously radiated in the form of visible-light traveling waves, manifesting as colored glow.

**N2T mechanism**: external high-energy free nodes are injected into the atmospheric node network, causing stress in some regions to reach the intermediate threshold, with continuous radiation without violent discharge.

## IV. Lightning: Violent Release When Stress Exceeds the Threshold

When local stress accumulation in the atmosphere exceeds the threshold $\theta_{\text{lightning}}$:

- Edges that were previously weakly connected or disconnected between nodes are forcibly established;
- A high-energy discharge channel forms;
- Stress is rapidly released along the channel, manifesting as intense light and sound.

**N2T mechanism**: stress accumulation exceeds the threshold; the node network undergoes批量 edge activation; stress is violently released along the channel.

## V. Ball Lightning: A Residual-Stress Metastable State After Discharge

After the lightning discharge ends, some regions may retain locked residual stress:

- Stress is not completely released; the node network is in a locally high-stress metastable state;
- These regions form self-constrained closed boundaries in space;
- Internal residual stress is slowly released, manifesting as a self-constrained luminous sphere;
- When the stress is fully released or the boundary is disturbed and broken, the sphere disappears or explodes.

**N2T mechanism**: after discharge, some regions enter a high-stress metastable state, forming self-constrained boundaries with slow release of residual stress.

## VII. Summary

In the N2T framework, atmospheric electrical phenomena form a complete spectrum of stress release:

- Extremely low stress $\rightarrow$ smooth light propagation;
- Low-moderate stress $\rightarrow$ airglow, aurora;
- High stress $\rightarrow$ lightning;
- Residual high stress $\rightarrow$ ball lightning.

They all share the same underlying mechanism: **the degree of edge activation and release in the node network is determined by the local stress level. Under different scales and stress conditions, the macroscopic phenomena differ, but the underlying logic is completely consistent.**
