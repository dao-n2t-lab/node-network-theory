In the N2T framework, dark energy is reinterpreted as: the global residual strain that the node network cannot completely eliminate during the stress minimization process.

This article presents the N2T framework's complete geometric explanation of dark energy from four perspectives: the origin of residual strain, the macroscopic manifestation of dark energy, the comparison with the standard model, and the unified relationship with cosmic expansion.

## I. Why Does Residual Strain Exist?

In the N2T framework, the node network is driven by stress minimization. Ideally, the global stress should tend toward zero. But in reality, the node network cannot fully reach a zero-stress state, for the following reasons:

### 1. The Finiteness of the Total Number of Nodes

The number of nodes is finite. In a finite network, the phase differences between nodes cannot be optimized simultaneously on all edges—there will always be regions where local stress cannot be completely eliminated.

This is like covering a sphere with a finite number of points—some areas will always be more "tight" or "loose" than others.

### 2. Phase Constraints on Closed Paths

On any closed path in the node network, the phase must satisfy a periodic condition (the sum of phases must be an integer multiple of $2\pi$).

When there are multiple sets of closed paths of different scales, the phase constraints of these paths may not be simultaneously satisfiable—resulting in stress that cannot be reduced to zero on all paths at once.

### 3. The Irreversibility of Large-Scale Structures

After structures such as galaxies and galaxy clusters form, a large number of nodes are locked into local stress balances and cannot return to a globally uniform state. This "locked" state manifests as persistent residual strain at large scales.

**Residual strain is an inherent property of the node network, not an accidental "deviation."**

## II. How Does Residual Strain Manifest as Dark Energy?

### 2.1 Macroscopic Effects of Residual Strain

Residual strain in the node network produces two macroscopic effects:

1. **Uniform global stretching**: residual strain keeps the global average edge length at a level slightly above the initial average spacing $d_0$，manifesting as a persistent tendency toward expansion. Here $d_0$ is the average spacing of nodes in the initial state of the universe—the baseline reference length of edges in a stress-free state;
2. **Spatial uniformity**: residual strain is uniformly distributed across the entire network, without tending to accumulate in any particular direction or form structures.

These two effects correspond precisely to the two observational characteristics of dark energy:

- **Driving accelerated expansion**: uniform global stretching corresponds to the observed accelerated expansion of the universe;
- **Spatial uniformity**: residual strain does not accumulate, corresponding to the uniform distribution of dark energy.

### 2.2 Correspondence with Vacuum Energy

In the standard model, dark energy is often interpreted as "vacuum energy." In the N2T framework:

| Standard Model Concept | N2T Framework Correspondence |
|:---|:---|
| Vacuum energy | Residual strain of the node network |
| Zero-point energy | The minimum amplitude of node vibration |
| Vacuum density | The accumulation of residual strain per unit volume |
| Uniformity of dark energy | Residual strain uniformly distributed across the entire network |

### 2.3 The Source of Dark Energy's "Negative Pressure"

In the standard model, dark energy has negative pressure ($w \approx -1$)。 In the N2T framework, this property comes from:

- The response of residual strain to edge-length deviation is "stretching" rather than "compression";
- When the node network is stretched, the residual strain actually decreases (stress tends toward zero);
- This behavior of "more stretching leads to more relaxation" is macroscopically equivalent to negative pressure.

## III. Residual Strain and Cosmic Expansion

### 3.1 From Expansion to Accelerated Expansion

In the N2T framework, cosmic expansion consists of two phases:

| Phase | Dominant Mechanism | Expansion Characteristic |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Early expansion | Driven by node density gradients | Decelerating expansion (matter-dominated) |
| Late expansion | Dominated by residual strain | Accelerating expansion (dark-energy-dominated) |

After structure formation has consumed most of the node density gradients, residual strain becomes the dominant component of the global stress distribution and begins to drive accelerated cosmic expansion.

### 3.2 The Evolution of Residual Strain

Residual strain is not constant but changes with cosmic evolution:

- When nodes condense into material structures, part of the residual strain is "absorbed" $\rightarrow$ dark energy density decreases;
- When structures disintegrate or matter redistributes, residual strain is released $\rightarrow$ dark energy density increases.

**Dark energy density is not constant, but a reflection of changes in the node network state.**

## VI. Testable Inferences

1. **Dark energy density changes with the process of structure formation**: during active structure formation, more residual strain is "absorbed," and dark energy density is lower; during periods of slower structure formation, residual strain begins to dominate, and dark energy density is relatively higher;

2. **The ratio of dark energy to matter density is not constant**: as matter becomes locked into structures, the proportion of nodes participating in residual strain changes, and the ratio of dark energy density to matter density evolves over time;

3. **Accelerated cosmic expansion will not continue forever**: when residual strain is fully absorbed by structure formation, the growth of the global average edge length will level off.
