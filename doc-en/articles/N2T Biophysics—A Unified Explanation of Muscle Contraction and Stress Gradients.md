## I. Muscle Cells as Node Networks

### 1.1 The Node Network Structure of Muscle Fibers

In the N2T framework, muscle fibers are described as a highly ordered node network:

- Actin nodes are arranged into thin filaments, forming "tracks" in the network;
- Myosin nodes are arranged into thick filaments, forming "sliding units" in the network;
- Actin nodes and myosin nodes are connected by dynamically adjustable edges.

These edges are not fixed—they can be established (binding), maintained (locked), and broken (dissociation), driven by neural signals and the phase modulation of ATP.

### 1.2 Resting State: Low-Stress Network

When the muscle is relaxed, the phase differences between actin nodes and myosin nodes are small, and the stress on the edges is at a low level. The entire muscle fiber node network is in a "loosely locked" state, with no dominant stress gradient direction.

## II. The Stress Mechanism of Muscle Contraction

### 2.1 Phase Locking $\rightarrow$ Edge Compression

When a neural signal reaches the muscle cell, calcium ions are released, changing the phase state of actin nodes. This increases the phase difference between actin nodes and myosin nodes, significantly raising the stress on the edges.

In the N2T framework, when the myosin head establishes an edge connection with an actin node, if the phase difference between the two is close to $180^\circ$ (opposite phase), the stress on the edge reaches its maximum—this is the microscopic source of "contractile force."

### 2.2 Stress Accumulation $\rightarrow$ Net Stress Gradient

When a large number of actin-myosin edges simultaneously enter a high-stress state, the entire muscle fiber node network generates a net stress gradient along the direction of contraction:

- At one end of the contraction direction: stress increases (edges are compressed);
- At the other end: stress decreases (edges tend toward natural length).

The node network (muscle fiber) moves along the stress gradient direction—that is, it contracts toward the end of lower stress.

### 2.3 Stress Conduction $\rightarrow$ Bone Movement

Both ends of the muscle are connected to the skeletal node network through tendons. When a contractile stress gradient is generated inside the muscle, this stress gradient is transmitted through the tendon node network to the bone surface, causing the skeletal node cluster to move along the stress direction.

In the N2T framework, **bone movement is not "pulled," but moves along the stress gradient direction generated by muscle contraction.**

## III. The Phase Modulation Role of ATP

| Standard Physiological Explanation | N2T Framework Correspondence |
|:---|:---|
| ATP hydrolysis releases chemical energy | ATP molecules couple with myosin nodes, changing their vibration amplitude and phase, altering the stress state on the edges |
| Myosin head binds to actin | Myosin nodes establish edges with actin nodes, with phase difference close to opposite phase |
| Cross-bridge swinging generates tension | Stress on the edges accumulates along the contraction direction, generating a net stress gradient |
| Muscle shortening generates force | The muscle cell node network contracts along the stress gradient direction |
| Dissociation after ATP hydrolysis | ATP consumption causes phase difference changes, edges break, and the network rebounds to a low-stress state |

**ATP is not "fuel," but a "phase modulator."** It changes the vibrational state of node clusters, enabling the stress gradient to be initiated, maintained, and released. Neural signals determine "when to start," and ATP determines "whether it can be sustained."

## IV. Unified Comparison with Other Forms of Motion

In the N2T framework, all macroscopic motion comes from the directional regulation of stress gradients:

| Form of Motion | Node Network | Source of Stress Gradient |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Muscle contraction | Muscle fiber node network | Actin-myosin phase locking |
| Flagellar/ciliary motion | Microtubule node network | Phase difference driving of tubulin nodes |
| Cell migration | Cytoskeleton node network | Stress from actin polymerization/depolymerization |
| Animal walking/running | Multi-muscle node network coordination | Temporal regulation of multi-directional stress gradients |
| Physical propulsion (propeller) | Fluid/solid node network | Phase difference driving between blades and fluid |

They all share the same underlying logic: **node clusters move along the direction of stress gradients, and the stress gradient is generated by phase differences and edge length deviations between nodes.**

## V. Summary

In the N2T framework, the essence of muscle contraction is:

**The muscle cell node network generates high-stress regions through phase locking, forms a net stress gradient along the direction of contraction, and moves the node cluster (bone) along the stress gradient direction. Neural signals trigger changes in phase states, and ATP maintains the sustained operation of the stress gradient by modulating node vibrations.**

This explanation unifies "biological motion" and "physical propulsion" under the same underlying mechanism—they are both motions of node networks driven by stress gradients, differing only in scale and node type.

**Muscle contraction is not a "conversion of chemical energy into mechanical energy," but the directional accumulation and release of phase stress in the node network.**
