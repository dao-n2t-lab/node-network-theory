## Two Key Observations

### Observation 1: Alternating Growth of Scale and Node Count

- Scale does not monotonically increase during structure formation—it collapses during fusion/collapse stages (Hydrogen gas cloud $\rightarrow$ Star $\rightarrow$ Neutron star $\rightarrow$ Black hole), then expands again at galactic and cosmic scales;
- Node count monotonically increases as levels rise (from 3 quarks to $10^{80}$ nucleon nodes in the observable universe), but each new level does not add new entities—rather, it reorganizes the connection topology of existing nodes.

### Observation 2: Stepwise Emergence of Properties and Behaviors

- The properties of each level are collective manifestations of the topology and vibration modes of the lower-level node network;
- The behaviors of each level are the result of "mode switching" in the lower-level node network driven by stress minimization;
- The leap from atoms to life is the largest leap in property hierarchy—from a passive physical network to an active self-preservation goal system.

## I. Fundamental Unit Layer

### Node (Level 0)

- **Scale**: $\sim 10^{-15}$ m
- **Constituent**: 1 fundamental unit
- **Key Properties**: Position, phase, vibration amplitude
- **Key Behaviors**: Receive-update-send (three rules)
- **Driving Mechanism**: Vibration (the node's own activity)

The node is the most fundamental entity in the N2T framework. It has no mass, no size, no internal structure—only position, phase, and vibration amplitude. All higher-level structures emerge stepwise from nodes through edge connections and vibrational coupling.

### Quark (Level 1)

- **Scale**: $\sim 10^{-15}$ m
- **Constituent**: 3 nodes forming a triangular ring
- **Key Properties**: Color charge ($0^\circ/120^\circ/240^\circ$ phase differences), flavor (up/down/charm/strange/top/bottom)
- **Key Behaviors**: Phase locking, color confinement
- **Driving Mechanism**: Standing-wave condition

In the triangular closed loop, the three nodes maintain fixed phase differences of $0^\circ$, $120^\circ$, and $240^\circ$. When the three phase differences sum to $360^\circ$, the overall state is "colorless," corresponding to the SU(3) symmetry of quark color charge. The overall vibration frequency of the triangle determines the quark's flavor, and asymmetric vibration modes correspond to excited states.

## II. Nucleon Layer

### Proton (Level 2)

- **Scale**: $\sim 10^{-15}$ m
- **Constituent**: 7 nodes (three quark rings uud sharing one central node)
- **Key Properties**: Charge $+1$, mass $1.672\times 10^{-27}$ kg, spin $1/2$
- **Key Behaviors**: Strong-force edge connection, phase synchronization
- **Driving Mechanism**: Three-ring phase synchronization

Three quark rings (up, up, down quarks) stack into a tetrahedral structure by sharing a central node, presenting a "colorless" state. The phase sum of the three rings is always $360^\circ$, and the overall vibration frequency determines the nucleon mass.

### Neutron (Level 3)

- **Scale**: $\sim 10^{-15}$ m
- **Constituent**: 7 nodes (three quark rings udd sharing one central node)
- **Key Properties**: Charge $0$, mass $1.675\times 10^{-27}$ kg, spin $1/2$
- **Key Behaviors**: Strong-force edge connection, β-decay tendency
- **Driving Mechanism**: Three-ring phase synchronization (slightly looser than proton)

Three quark rings (up, down, down quarks) stack into a tetrahedral structure by sharing a central node, presenting a "colorless" state. The overall vibration frequency is slightly higher than that of the proton (due to the extra down quark's vibrational mass contribution), and it decays into a proton in about 15 minutes in its free state.

## III. Atomic and Molecular Layer

### Atomic Nucleus (Level 4)

- **Scale**: $\sim 10^{-15}$ m (radius $1.2A^{1/3}$ fm)
- **Constituent**: Nucleon cluster, total node count $7A$ ($A$ = mass number)
- **Key Properties**: Mass number $A$, charge number $Z$, magic numbers (2,8,20,28,50,82,126)
- **Key Behaviors**: Nuclear force, decay, fission/fusion
- **Driving Mechanism**: Internal-external stress ratio $R$

Nucleons are connected through strong-force edges, forming nested tetrahedral grids. Magic numbers correspond to perfectly symmetric closed node shells, where $R = \sigma_{\text{internal}} / \sigma_{\text{external}}$ is exactly 1, placing the nucleus in the ideal state of stress minimization. The phase sum of any closed loop must be zero (the extension of "color confinement" to the nuclear scale).

### Atom (Level 5)

- **Scale**: $\sim 10^{-10}$ m (Bohr radius order)
- **Constituent**: Atomic nucleus + shell-layer hexagonal grid, total node count $7A + N_{\text{shell}}$
- **Key Properties**: Element type, electron energy levels, ionization energy, atomic radius
- **Key Behaviors**: Electron standing waves, chemical bonds
- **Driving Mechanism**: Phase-geometric dual-stress coupling

The nucleus couples with the shell-layer hexagonal grid through radial edges, and electron standing waves form eigenvibration modes on the shell-layer grid. The number of nodal surfaces on the spherical grid determines orbital shapes (s, p, d, f), and the number of radial nodal surfaces determines the principal quantum number $n$. The energy level spacing $E_n \propto 1/n^2$ is a geometric necessity of the spherical grid standing wave.

### Molecule (Level 6)

- **Scale**: $\sim 10^{-9}$ m
- **Constituent**: Multiple atom node clusters connected through shared edges
- **Key Properties**: Bond order, molecular orbitals, electronegativity
- **Key Behaviors**: Edge sharing, chemical reactions
- **Driving Mechanism**: Shared-edge stress minimization

Two atomic shell-layer hexagonal grids couple through edge sharing to form a molecule. The number of shared edges determines bond order (single/double/triple bonds), the degree of phase matching determines bond strength, and the merged grid's standing-wave modes form molecular orbitals. Electronegativity can be expressed as the ratio of nuclear nodes to shell-layer vertices.

## IV. Celestial and Cosmic Layer

### Hydrogen Gas Cloud (Level 7)

- **Scale**: $10^{-3} \sim 10^{12}$ m (cross-scale)
- **Constituent**: Large number of hydrogen atoms forming a diffuse network through weak connections, $\sim 10^{60}$ atom node clusters
- **Key Properties**: Density, temperature, pressure
- **Key Behaviors**: Free diffusion, slow gravitational aggregation
- **Driving Mechanism**: Weak connections, density fluctuations

A hydrogen gas cloud consists of a large number of hydrogen atoms forming a diffuse network through weak connections. When node density fluctuations reach a certain threshold, local stress gradients drive node clusters to gradually aggregate, providing the material foundation for star formation. This is the precursor stage of star formation.

### Star (Level 8)

- **Scale**: $10^6 \sim 10^9$ m
- **Constituent**: Shell-layer shared grid + fusion core, $\sim 10^{56}$ node clusters participating in core resonance
- **Key Properties**: Mass, temperature, fusion state, luminosity
- **Key Behaviors**: Network restructuring, fusion
- **Driving Mechanism**: Resonance amplification, $N_{\text{shared}} \approx 1/\alpha \approx 137$

In the high-density environment of a stellar core, atomic nuclei's shell-layer grids overlap, and vibration waves coherently superimpose and amplify. When the number of shared grid nodes reaches approximately 137, local stress exceeds the combination threshold, triggering fusion. Iron is the endpoint of fusion because the internal-external stress ratio of the iron nucleus reaches the global minimum. Stellar internal layering (core/radiative zone/convective zone) corresponds to hydrogen atoms' mode switching at different depths.

### Neutron Star (Level 9)

- **Scale**: $\sim 10^4$ m
- **Constituent**: Shell-layer grid completely collapsed, neutron node clusters in direct contact, $\sim 10^{57}$ neutron nodes
- **Key Properties**: Density limit, ultra-strong magnetic field (up to $10^{11}$ T), rotation
- **Key Behaviors**: Pulsed radiation, magnetospheric activity
- **Driving Mechanism**: Shell-layer compression, spin order freezing

The nuclear shell-layer grid is completely crushed, and electrons are forced to combine with protons to form neutron node clusters. When a neutron star rotates at high speed, surface topological defects periodically sweep across the line of sight, emitting coherent phase beams (pulse signals). The spin directions of a large number of neutron nodes are frozen into a highly ordered arrangement during shell-layer collapse, generating an ultra-strong magnetic field.

### Black Hole (Level 10)

- **Scale**: $10^3 \sim 10^6$ m (horizon scale)
- **Constituent**: Node cluster density reaches topological saturation, all nodes fall into极限压缩
- **Key Properties**: Mass, angular momentum, charge (three parameters)
- **Key Behaviors**: Global phase synchronization, Hawking radiation (weak)
- **Driving Mechanism**: Geometric stress overwhelms phase stress

When node cluster density exceeds the critical value, geometric stress $S_g$ completely overwhelms phase stress $S_\theta$, and the node cluster enters a topological saturation state. The event horizon corresponds to the position where the wave speed $v_w$ on network edges drops to zero. The standard model's singularity is eliminated—the node cluster enters a fully phase-synchronized static state. Hawking radiation is the stress leakage from edge breakage near the horizon, with intensity proportional to the boundary curvature.

### Galaxy (Level 11)

- **Scale**: $10^{20} \sim 10^{21}$ m
- **Constituent**: Node density gradient (shared nodes + free nodes), $\sim 10^{60}$ node clusters
- **Key Properties**: Rotation curve, dark matter distribution, morphology (spiral/elliptical/irregular)
- **Key Behaviors**: Density-gradient driving, rotation
- **Driving Mechanism**: Phase difference between free and shared nodes

Visible matter (shared nodes) concentrates at the galactic center, while free nodes (dark matter) distribute in the periphery, forming a low-density large-scale halo. Free nodes generate additional centripetal drag through long-range geometric stress, keeping the rotation curve flat. Galactic morphology is determined by the phase difference distribution between shared and free nodes.

### Universe (Level 12)

- **Scale**: $\sim 10^{26}$ m (observable universe radius)
- **Constituent**: Global node network, $\sim 10^{80}$ nucleon nodes
- **Key Properties**: Hubble constant $H_0 \approx 70$ km/s/Mpc, dark energy density $\approx 10^{-9}$ J/m³, CMB temperature $2.725$ K
- **Key Behaviors**: Global average edge length change
- **Driving Mechanism**: Residual strain, periodic oscillation

The expansion of the universe corresponds to the growth of the global average edge length. Dark energy is the global residual of phase strain across the entire network; dark matter consists of free nodes that have not formed standing-wave modes; and the cosmic microwave background is the frozen remnant of early global vibration modes. The universe undergoes periodic oscillations of "expansion-restructuring-contraction-rebound," driven by the cycle of stress concentration and release.

## VI. Key Progressive Patterns

### Pattern 1: Stepwise Emergence of Properties and Behaviors

The properties of each level are collective manifestations of the topology and vibration modes of the lower-level node network. The behaviors of each level are the result of "mode switching" in the lower-level node network driven by stress minimization.

### Pattern 2: Alternating Growth of Scale and Node Count

- Scale does not monotonically increase during structure formation—it collapses during fusion/collapse stages (Hydrogen gas cloud $\rightarrow$ Star $\rightarrow$ Neutron star $\rightarrow$ Black hole), then expands again at galactic and cosmic scales;
- Node count monotonically increases as levels rise (from 3 quarks to $10^{80}$ nucleon nodes in the observable universe), but each new level does not add new entities—rather, it reorganizes the connection topology of existing nodes.

### Pattern 3: Different Level-Dependent Origins of the Same Property

The same physical quantity manifests differently at different levels:

- "Mass" is Higgs strain at the microscopic level and node density at the macroscopic level;
- "Force" is phase stress at the microscopic level and stress gradient at the macroscopic level;
- "Memory" is electron standing-wave modes in atoms and phase freezing in neuron node networks in life.
