This article presents the unified explanation of liquid networks in the framework from two aspects: internal behaviors (flow, viscosity) and boundary behaviors (surface tension, capillarity, wettability, evaporation/boiling, vacuum response).

## I. The Essence of Liquid: Node Cluster "Turning" and Connection Reconfiguration

In a liquid, each node cluster only needs to "turn slightly" to break its edge connection with its current neighbor and establish a new edge connection with another adjacent node cluster. This continuous cycle of "breaking-reforming" is the underlying mechanism of the liquid state.

The conditions for liquid formation are:

- The vibration amplitude is in the range of "able to break old connections but not sufficiently detached from the network";
- The vibration frequency is moderate, allowing node clusters to continuously attempt new connections.

**Macroscopic manifestation**: fluidity, incompressibility, no fixed shape.

## II. Viscosity: The Delay Effect of Node Cluster "Turning"

Viscosity describes the "sliding resistance" between different layers when a liquid flows:

- **Low viscosity**: node clusters can quickly break old connections and establish new ones;
- **High viscosity**: node clusters take longer to "turn," creating flow resistance.

When temperature rises, the vibration amplitude of node clusters increases, the "turning" speed accelerates, and viscosity decreases. When molecular structures are complex, node clusters are larger and have more connection points, requiring more complex "turning" to complete reconnection, and viscosity increases.

**Viscosity is the ratio of the time required for node clusters to "turn" to the flow speed.**

## III. Surface Tension: Constrained "Turning" Degrees of Freedom of Surface Node Clusters

Inside a liquid, each node cluster can "turn" in any direction to establish connections with neighbors above, below, left, and right. But at the liquid surface, there are no neighboring nodes above the node cluster, and its available "turning" directions are cut off.

- Surface node clusters can only establish connections toward the interior of the liquid, tending to move inward;
- This inward net stress manifests macroscopically as surface tension.

**Surface tension is not a "force," but the stress concentration generated by the system to maintain connection density after the "turning" degrees of freedom of surface node clusters are constrained.**

## IV. Capillarity: Surface Node Clusters "Climbing" Along Solid Surfaces

When a liquid contacts the wall of a narrow tube, the surface node clusters also face the node network of the solid surface:

- If the liquid-solid connection strength is greater than the liquid-liquid connection strength, surface node clusters preferentially move toward the solid surface;
- They establish connections upward along the solid surface, simultaneously dragging the entire liquid surface upward, until the weight of the liquid column balances the surface stress.

**The height of capillary rise depends on the degree of phase matching between the surface node clusters and the solid surface nodes.**

## V. Wettability: The "Preference" of Surface Node Clusters on Solid Surfaces

Wettability describes whether a liquid "spreads out" or "beads up" on a solid surface:

| Connection Type | Wetting Type | Contact Angle | Macroscopic Manifestation |
|:---|:---|:---:|:---|
| Liquid-solid $>$ liquid-liquid | Wetting | $< 90^\circ$ | Liquid spreads out |
| Liquid-solid $\approx$ liquid-liquid | Neutral | $\approx 90^\circ$ | Hemispherical |
| Liquid-solid $<$ liquid-liquid | Non-wetting | $> 90^\circ$ | Beads up |

**The essence of wettability is the phase choice of surface node clusters between "establishing edges with solid nodes" and "establishing edges with liquid nodes."**

## VI. Evaporation and Boiling: Node Clusters "Escaping" from the Liquid-Phase Network

Evaporation and boiling describe the process of liquid-phase node clusters detaching from the liquid-phase network and entering the gas phase:

- **Evaporation**: occurs at the liquid surface, where surface layer node clusters break their connections with the liquid-phase network one by one and enter the gas phase. Can occur at any temperature;
- **Boiling**: occurs inside the liquid phase, where a large number of node clusters undergo chain fracture after reaching the threshold, forming bubble nuclei that rapidly expand and rise. Requires a specific temperature (boiling point).

## VII. Liquid in Vacuum: Network Response After External Stress Removal

In the N2T framework, the vaporization of liquid in a vacuum is a direct manifestation of the boundary response of the liquid-phase node network after external stress is removed.

### Ground Boiling vs Space Evaporation

| Condition | Mechanism | Manifestation |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Ground (with atmospheric pressure) | The external air node network provides a strong phase reference; liquid-phase nodes must overcome atmospheric pressure stress to break | Batch fracture after heating to boiling point (boiling) |
| Space (vacuum) | The external air node network is removed; the energy threshold for liquid-phase node escape is greatly reduced | Surface layer nodes slowly escape (evaporation), no batch fracture |

### Free Nodes in Vacuum: Why Liquid Does Not Instantly Vaporize

Even when a container is evacuated to high vacuum, a certain amount of free nodes still exists above the liquid surface (from residual gas, container wall release, and liquid surface escape). These free nodes form a thin layer of weakly connected network near the liquid surface, providing a weak phase reference field for the liquid-phase nodes.

When the external air is removed, the strong phase reference provided by the air node network is removed, and the energy threshold for liquid-phase node escape is greatly reduced. But because residual free nodes still exist, the liquid-phase nodes still face an extremely low but non-zero energy threshold, preventing chain escape of deep-layer nodes.

Therefore, liquid in a vacuum does not instantly vaporize completely, but manifests as slow escape of surface layer nodes, continuous decrease in liquid-phase temperature, and eventual dynamic equilibrium between the liquid surface and the residual free nodes.

### Summary

In the N2T framework:

- **Boiling** is an energy-driven chain fracture of the node network, requiring continuous energy input;
- **Liquid in space** in a vacuum, although external stress has been removed, lacks a continuous heat source, so the vibration amplitude of nodes continuously decays, allowing only selective fracture of surface layer nodes (evaporation), unable to maintain internal batch fracture (boiling).

## VIII. Solution Formation: Mixed Networks of Multiple Node Cluster Types

Solution formation describes how different types of node clusters establish stable mixed networks in the liquid phase:

- A-B connection strength is comparable to A-A and B-B, with phase matching $\rightarrow$ solution (uniformly mixed);
- A-B connection strength is weaker than A-A/B-B $\rightarrow$ mixture (layered or precipitated);
- A-B phase difference is large or connection strength does not match $\rightarrow$ unable to form a stable mixture.

**Solution formation is the result of phase matching and connection strength competition between different node clusters, not an inherent property of a specific "solvent."**

## IX. Summary

All macroscopic behaviors of liquids—viscosity, surface tension, capillarity, wettability, evaporation, boiling, vacuum response, and solution formation—can be traced back in the N2T framework to the basic behavior of node clusters "establishing-breaking-reestablishing" connections.

They all share the same underlying mechanism, differing only in the boundary conditions and vibration states of the node clusters.

| Phenomenon | N2T Mechanism |
|:---|:---|
| Viscosity | Time required for node clusters to "turn" |
| Surface tension | Constrained "turning" degrees of freedom of surface node clusters |
| Capillarity | Surface node clusters "climbing" along solid surfaces |
| Wettability | Phase competition between liquid-solid and liquid-liquid connections |
| Evaporation | Surface layer node clusters escaping one by one |
| Boiling | Chain fracture of internal node clusters |
| Vacuum response | Network rebalancing after external stress removal |
| Solution formation | Phase matching and connection competition between different node clusters |
