## I. The Speed of Light $c$: The Upper Limit of Information Propagation

### 1.1 Emergence Relation

$$c = d_0 \cdot \omega_0$$

### 1.2 Cosmological Significance

The speed of light is the maximum speed of traveling-wave propagation in the node network. It determines:

- **The extent of causal boundaries**: the light-cone structure corresponds to the limit of stress propagation in the node network;
- **The upper limit of structure formation speed**: density perturbations cannot propagate through the node network faster than $c$;
- **The reference point for cosmic expansion**: the Hubble radius $c/H_0$ defines the boundary of the observable universe.

In the N2T framework, the speed of light is not the "ultimate speed limit of the universe"—it is the natural wave speed of stress-free edges in the node network. If the node density changes (as in the higher-density early universe), $d_0$ and $\omega_0$ may differ, and the speed of light may also differ.

**The speed of light is a parameter of the node network's state, not an absolute, unchanging background.**

## II. Planck's Constant $h$: The Minimum Action Unit of Vibration

### 2.1 Emergence Relation

$$h = \omega_0 \cdot d_0^2 \cdot \alpha$$

### 2.2 Cosmological Significance

Planck's constant is the smallest "action unit" in the node network—that is, the minimum energy-time product required for a node to complete one phase update.

It determines:

- **The scale of quantum effects**: when a system's action approaches $h$，the discreteness of the node network becomes significant;
- **The uncertainty relation**: the coupling between node phase and stress naturally produces the relation $\Delta E \cdot \Delta t \sim h$；
- **The critical threshold for phase transitions**: when the energy density approaches the order of $h$，phase-transition behavior of the node network (such as vacuum phase transitions) becomes possible.

In the N2T framework, $h$ is not the "entrance to the microscopic world"—it is the critical scale at which the node network switches from "continuous manifestation" to "discrete manifestation."

## III. The Gravitational Constant $G$: The Coupling Coefficient Between Node Density and Stress

### 3.1 Emergence Relation

$$G = \frac{d_0^2 \cdot \omega_0^2}{N_0 \cdot \alpha}$$

### 3.2 Cosmological Significance

The gravitational constant converts node density into observable gravitational effects. It determines:

- **The critical density for structure formation**: when the local node density exceeds $\rho_{\text{crit}} \sim H_0^2/G$，self-aggregation of node clusters begins;
- **The geometric curvature of the universe**: the ratio $\Omega = \rho / \rho_{\text{crit}}$ of node density to critical density determines the geometric form of the universe (open, closed, or flat);
- **The unification of gravity and inertia**: the directionality of density gradients in the node network simultaneously produces "gravitational attraction" and "inertial resistance"—both are different manifestations of the same stress gradient.

In the N2T framework, $G$ is not the "strength of gravity"—it is the conversion coefficient between node density and spatial curvature.

## IV. The Fine-Structure Constant $\alpha$: The Matter-Node Coupling Strength

### 4.1 Emergence Relation

$$\alpha = \left(\frac{g_{\text{spin}}}{g_{\text{charge}}}\right)^2 \cdot \left(\text{three-ring superposition correction}\right) \approx \frac{1}{137}$$

### 4.2 Cosmological Significance

The fine-structure constant determines:

- **The stability of atoms**: the phase coupling strength of the hydrogen atom is precisely such that the atom is in a "stable but triggerable" critical state;
- **The complexity of chemical reactions**: the value of $\alpha$ determines how many types of molecular structures atoms can form;
- **The gentleness of stellar energy release**: if $\alpha$ were slightly larger, stellar fusion would be more violent; if slightly smaller, fusion would be slower or even fail to ignite.

In the N2T framework, $\alpha$ is not the "strength of the electromagnetic force"—it is the topological coupling ratio of standing-wave modes in the hexagonal loop, determining how elementary particles organize into stable structures.

## V. The Electric Charge $e$: The Divergence of Phase Stress

### 5.1 Emergence Relation

$$e = \sqrt{4\pi \varepsilon_0 \hbar c \alpha}$$

### 5.2 Cosmological Significance

Electric charge is the divergence of the phase stress $S_\theta$ in the node network. It determines:

- **The coupling of matter and light**: the existence of charge makes interaction between traveling waves and node clusters possible;
- **The asymmetry of positive and negative charges**: the directionality of phase differences in the node network naturally produces the distinction between positive and negative charges;
- **The charge neutrality of the universe**: the total phase divergence is zero across the entire network, corresponding to the net charge of the universe being zero.

In the N2T framework, charge is not a "property of matter"—it is the macroscopic manifestation of phase gradients in the node network.

## VIII. Summary

In the N2T framework, fundamental constants are not "parameters of the universe," but manifestations of the node network's state at different scales:

- **$c$** is the limit of information propagation;
- **$h$** is the critical scale between discrete and continuous;
- **$G$** is the conversion coefficient between node density and spatial curvature;
- **$\alpha$** is the organizational strength of material structure;
- **$e$** is the macroscopic manifestation of phase gradients.

Their values determine that the universe can form structures, stars can burn, and life can appear. But they are not "designed to be this way"—they are the natural result jointly determined by geometry and stress when the node network forms stable standing-wave structures in three-dimensional space.

**The universe is not defined by "constants," but by the "geometry of the node network." Constants are merely the projection of geometry at the macroscopic scale.**
