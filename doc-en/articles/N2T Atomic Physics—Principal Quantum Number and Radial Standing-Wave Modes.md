## I. The Geometric Definition of Radial Nodal Surfaces

### 1.1 What Is a Radial Nodal Surface?

A radial nodal surface is a vibration node of the shell-layer node network in the **radial direction**—that is, at that radius, the radial vibration amplitude of the node network is zero.

It corresponds to: **the position where the standing wave's phase flips in the radial direction.**

In standard quantum mechanics, the number of radial nodal surfaces is $n-1$. In the N2T framework, this corresponds to the number of times the radial vibration wave propagating outward from the nucleus is "reflected."

### 1.2 Radial Nodal Surfaces in the Node Network

| Principal Quantum Number $n$ | Number of Radial Nodal Surfaces | Radial Vibration Mode of the Shell-Layer Grid |
|:---:|:---:|:---|
| 1 | 0 | No nodal surfaces; amplitude decays monotonically outward from the nucleus |
| 2 | 1 | One nodal surface; amplitude first increases, then decreases, then extends outward |
| 3 | 2 | Two nodal surfaces; amplitude oscillates twice before extending outward |

The greater the number of radial nodal surfaces, the larger the radius range of the electron cloud and the higher the corresponding energy.

## II. Conditions for the Formation of Radial Standing-Wave Modes

In the N2T framework, the formation of radial standing waves is determined by two factors:

### 2.1 Phase Reflection at the Nucleus

The nuclear surface is a high-density node cluster boundary. When a radial vibration wave propagating inward from the shell layer reaches the nuclear surface, it is reflected back. The incident and reflected waves superimpose in the radial direction, forming a standing wave.

The positions of the radial nodal surfaces are determined by the phase difference between the incident and reflected waves.

### 2.2 The Phase Condition for Radial Path Length

A stable radial standing-wave mode requires that the path length from the nuclear surface to the outer boundary of the shell layer and back satisfy the half-wavelength integer condition:

$$2 \cdot R_{\text{shell}} = n \cdot \frac{\lambda}{2}$$

where $R_{\text{shell}}$ is the effective radius of the shell-layer grid, and $n$ is the principal quantum number.

When this condition is satisfied, the radial standing wave forms stable phase locking at the nuclear surface and the outer boundary of the shell layer, and the shell-layer node network exhibits $n-1$ nodal surfaces in the radial direction.

## III. Radial Standing-Wave Modes for Different Principal Quantum Numbers

### 3.1 $n = 1$: Ground State, No Radial Nodal Surfaces

- Radial vibration distribution: monotonically decaying outward from the nucleus;
- Electron cloud extent: smallest;
- Energy: lowest (ground state);
- N2T mechanism: the radial path length satisfies $2R = \lambda/2$, with no nodal surfaces along the path.

### 3.2 $n = 2$: First Excited State, One Radial Nodal Surface

- Radial vibration distribution: first increases then decreases outward from the nucleus, crossing zero at a certain radius, then tending to zero at the outer boundary;
- Electron cloud extent: larger than $n = 1$;
- Energy: higher than the ground state;
- N2T mechanism: the radial path length satisfies $2R = \lambda$, with one nodal surface at $R/2$.

### 3.3 $n = 3$: Second Excited State, Two Radial Nodal Surfaces

- Radial vibration distribution: amplitude oscillates twice outward from the nucleus (increase $\rightarrow$ cross zero $\rightarrow$ increase $\rightarrow$ cross zero $\rightarrow$ tends to zero at the outer boundary);
- Electron cloud extent: even larger;
- Energy: even higher;
- N2T mechanism: the radial path length satisfies $2R = 3\lambda/2$, with two nodal surfaces at $R/3$ and $2R/3$.

## IV. Radial Standing Waves and Energy Levels

In standard quantum mechanics, the energy level formula is:

$$E_n \propto -\frac{1}{n^2}$$

In the N2T framework, this relationship comes from: **the "wavelength-radius" constraint of the standing wave on the radial path.**

When the principal quantum number $n$ increases:

- The radial path length $2R$ must accommodate $n$ half-wavelengths;
- The wavelength shortens $(\lambda \propto 1/n)$;
- The standing wave energy increases (the inverse relationship of $E \propto 1/\lambda^2 \propto n^2$).

**The $1/n^2$ law of energy-level spacing is a geometric necessity of the radial standing-wave mode.**
