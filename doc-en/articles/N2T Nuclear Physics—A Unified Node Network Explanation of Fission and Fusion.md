## I. The Unified Mechanism of Fission and Fusion: Redistribution of Stress Balance

In the N2T framework, the atomic nucleus is a node cluster in stress balance. The essence of nuclear reactions is the transition of the node network from one stress equilibrium state to another, releasing or absorbing excess stress.

- **Fission**: When the internal stress distribution of the atomic nucleus is uneven, the node cluster splits into two (or more) smaller node clusters, redistributing stress;
- **Fusion**: When multiple atomic nuclei approach each other and share shell-layer grids, the node clusters merge into a larger node cluster, with stress rebalanced.

**They are the same mechanism manifested in opposite directions.**

## II. Fission: Stress Release of the Node Network

### 2.1 Prerequisites for Fission

In the N2T framework, fission occurs under the following conditions:

1. The internal stress distribution of the nucleus is uneven (especially in heavy nuclei, where phase stress accumulates between protons);
2. The nucleus is struck by an external neutron, causing local stress to exceed the threshold;
3. The nucleus deforms, with stress concentrated on a "weak plane" of the node network.

When these conditions are met, the nuclear node cluster breaks along the direction of stress concentration—splitting into two smaller, more stable node clusters.

### 2.2 The Geometric Origin of Energy Release in Fission

During fission, the stress previously locked in the strong-force edges within the heavy nucleus is released:

1. When the heavy nucleus splits into two lighter nuclei, the shared edges between nucleons are broken;
2. The energy released from breaking edges propagates outward in the form of traveling waves (neutrons, photons);
3. The lighter nuclei have higher specific binding energy (lower node network stress), so the released energy exceeds the absorbed energy.

### 2.3 Stress Balance of Fission Products

The nuclei of fission products (such as helium, barium) are typically in node structures "close to magic numbers," with lower internal stress. This indicates that the node network automatically selects a more stable stress equilibrium state during fission.

In the N2T framework, **fission is not an "explosion," but a transfer of the node network from a high-stress state to a low-stress state.**

## III. Fusion: Merging of the Node Network

### 3.1 Prerequisites for Fusion

In the N2T framework, fusion occurs under the following conditions:

1. Multiple atomic nuclei approach each other under high temperature and high pressure (e.g., in a stellar core);
2. The shell-layer grids of the nuclei undergo sharing (forming a shared grid region);
3. The vibration waves on the shared grid undergo coherent superposition, causing local stress to exceed the merging threshold.

When these conditions are met, multiple nuclear node clusters merge into a larger node cluster.

### 3.2 The Geometric Origin of Energy Release in Fusion

During fusion, when multiple light nuclei merge into a heavier nucleus, the shared edges of the shell-layer grids are "internalized":

1. The shell-layer grids of hydrogen nuclei overlap in the shared region;
2. The shared edges are internalized as strong-force edges within the nucleus;
3. The stress energy released during internalization propagates outward in the form of traveling waves (photons).

In the N2T framework, **fusion is not "ignition," but a transfer of the node network from a low-density shared state to a high-density locked state.**

### 3.3 Why Is Iron the Endpoint of Fusion?

The iron nucleus has the highest specific binding energy—in the N2T framework, this means that the nuclear node network of iron is in the lowest stress state. Continuing fusion would form a node cluster with higher stress, rather than reducing stress.

**Iron is the endpoint of node network stress minimization, not because the iron atom itself is "particularly stable."**
