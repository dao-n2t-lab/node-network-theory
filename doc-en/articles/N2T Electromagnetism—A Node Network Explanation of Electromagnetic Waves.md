In standard physics, electromagnetic waves are described as oscillations of electric and magnetic fields that mutually excite each other and propagate through space. In the N2T framework, electromagnetic waves are reinterpreted as: traveling-wave modes of phase gradients in the node network—as the phase gradient propagates, its divergence component (electric field) and curl component (magnetic field) transform into each other, propagating through the node network at the speed of light.

This article presents the N2T framework's complete geometric explanation of electromagnetic waves from four perspectives: the traveling-wave mode of phase gradients, the coupling of electric and magnetic fields, the propagation mechanism of electromagnetic waves, and the relationship between photons and traveling waves.

## I. Traveling-Wave Mode of Phase Gradients

In the N2T framework, electromagnetic waves are defined as traveling waves of the phase gradient $\nabla \theta$ propagating through the node network.

When a charged node cluster vibrates, its phase distribution changes accordingly. This change propagates outward along the edges of the node network in the form of waves.

During propagation, the phase gradient carries two components:

- **Divergence component** $\nabla \cdot (\nabla \theta)$：corresponds to the electric field;
- **Curl component** $\nabla \times (\nabla \theta)$：corresponds to the magnetic field.

These two components are not independent, but different geometric manifestations of the same phase gradient.

## II. Mutual Transformation of Electric and Magnetic Fields

In the N2T framework, as the phase gradient propagates, its divergence and curl components transform into each other.

| Change in Phase Gradient | Effect Produced | Standard Physics Correspondence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Divergence component changes with time | Produces curl component | Changing electric field produces magnetic field (Maxwell-Ampere law) |
| Curl component changes with time | Produces divergence component | Changing magnetic field produces electric field (Faraday's law of induction) |

This mutual transformation is an inevitable consequence of the phase gradient traveling-wave mode—during propagation, divergence and curl alternately dominate, forming a self-sustaining propagation structure.

**Electromagnetic waves are not a causal chain of "electric field produces magnetic field, which then produces electric field," but the natural oscillation of the divergence and curl components of the phase gradient during propagation.**

## III. Propagation Mechanism of Electromagnetic Waves

### 3.1 Propagation Path

In the N2T framework, electromagnetic waves propagate along the edges of the node network. As the traveling-wave mode propagates, the phase gradient travels along the path direction, while the divergence and curl components oscillate perpendicular to this direction.

### 3.2 Wave Speed

The propagation speed of electromagnetic waves in the node network is determined by the natural edge length $L_0$ and the minimum propagation delay $\tau_{\min}$：

$$c = \frac{L_0}{\tau_{\min}}$$

When edges are at their natural length (stress-free), the wave speed reaches its maximum—the speed of light $c$. Any stress reduces the wave speed.

### 3.3 Energy Transmission

The energy carried by electromagnetic waves exists in the form of phase gradients. The energy density is proportional to the square of the phase gradient—both the divergence and curl components contribute to the energy of the electromagnetic wave.

## IV. Photons: The Quantized Manifestation of Traveling Waves

In the N2T framework, photons are defined as the quantized units of phase gradient traveling-wave modes during propagation.

| Standard Physics Concept | N2T Correspondence |
|:---|:---|
| Photon | Discretized unit of the phase gradient traveling-wave mode |
| Photon energy $E = h\nu$ | Frequency $\nu$ of the phase gradient traveling wave, determined by the traveling-wave mode |
| Photon spin | Polarization direction of the phase gradient traveling wave (left-handed/right-handed) |
| Photon momentum $p = E/c$ | Momentum of the phase gradient traveling wave propagating along edges |

In the node network, a photon's energy is determined by its frequency $\nu$——the higher the frequency, the faster the phase gradient oscillates, and the greater the energy carried. Photon spin corresponds to the rotation direction of the wave's polarization—left-handed or right-handed—consistent with the wave's polarization direction.

In the N2T framework, different frequency bands of the electromagnetic spectrum correspond to phase gradient traveling-wave modes manifesting in node networks at different scales:

| Band | Node Network Characteristic | Macroscopic Manifestation |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Radio waves | Low-frequency phase gradient traveling waves, long wavelength | Strong penetration, pronounced diffraction |
| Microwaves | Moderate low-frequency traveling waves | Can be absorbed by molecules (heating effect) |
| Infrared | Higher-frequency traveling waves, coupled with molecular vibrations | Thermal radiation |
| Visible light | Matches standing-wave frequencies of atomic shell-layer grids | Perceptible by vision |
| Ultraviolet | High-frequency traveling waves, can excite electron transitions | Ionization effects |
| X-rays | Very high-frequency traveling waves, coupled with inner-shell electrons | Penetrates matter |
| $\gamma$ rays | Extremely high-frequency traveling waves, coupled with nuclear energy levels | Nuclear-level ionizing radiation |

Different bands of electromagnetic waves are, in the N2T framework, the same type of phenomenon—phase gradient traveling-wave modes manifesting in node networks at different scales.

In the N2T framework, the essence of electromagnetic waves is:

**Traveling-wave modes of phase gradients in the node network. The divergence component (electric field) and curl component (magnetic field) alternately dominate during propagation, forming a self-sustaining traveling-wave structure.**
