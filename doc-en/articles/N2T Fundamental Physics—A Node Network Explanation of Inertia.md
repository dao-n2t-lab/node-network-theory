## I. The Position of Inertial Motion in Standard Physics

Newton's first law states: an object, if not acted upon by external forces, will remain at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line. This law cannot be "explained"—it is an axiom, the starting point of mechanics.

"Inertia" is treated as an intrinsic property of objects—the greater the mass, the greater the inertia. But standard physics does not answer "why objects maintain their state of motion"—it simply assumes it as a postulate.

In the N2T framework, we do not allow any "default assumptions." Inertia must have a source.

## II. The Underlying Mechanism of Inertial Motion

A macroscopic object (such as a particle or a planet) in the N2T framework is a node cluster—internal nodes connected by edges, with stable phase locking and stress distribution. When this node cluster is in a region with uniform node density and stress distribution, it experiences no net stress:

- The node network in front and the node network behind have the same stress state;
- The phase distribution of nodes is uniform, with no stress gradients;
- Traveling waves propagating along edges are neither impeded nor accelerated.

In this state, the overall motion of the node cluster requires no continuous energy input—because it experiences no "resisting" force. Its motion is the natural result of "continuing the current state," not of "being pushed."

**Inertial motion is the "free glide" of a node cluster in a stress-gradient-free region—it is not being pushed, but rather not being stopped.**

## III. Why Does Greater Mass Mean Greater Inertia?

In the N2T framework, mass is not a property of nodes themselves, but the stress intensity within a node cluster. A massive object means its internal edge connections are denser and its phase locking is tighter.

To change the state of motion of a massive object, a wider range of phase-locking stress must be overcome:

- When a force is applied, the external perturbation must penetrate the entire node cluster and readjust the internal phase distribution;
- If the phase locking inside the node cluster is strong, this adjustment requires greater external stress;
- This manifests as greater inertia—that is, greater difficulty in changing the state of motion.

**Greater mass = tighter internal phase locking = harder to change the state of motion.**

This relationship macroscopically manifests as Newton's second law $F = ma$，where $m$ is the macroscopic statistical average of the internal stress intensity of the node cluster.

## IV. Uniform Linear Motion and Traveling-Wave Propagation

In the N2T framework, uniform linear motion can be viewed as the stable propagation of traveling waves in a uniform node network:

- The overall direction of motion of the object node cluster corresponds to the direction of traveling-wave propagation in the node network;
- In a uniform network, traveling waves do not bend—their path is straight;
- In a uniform network, traveling waves neither accelerate nor decelerate—because no stress gradient consumes or adds to their energy.

**Uniform linear motion is the natural propagation path of a traveling wave in a uniform node network.** When a node cluster is in a uniform network, its traveling-wave mode propagates in a straight line, without spontaneously deviating or changing speed.
