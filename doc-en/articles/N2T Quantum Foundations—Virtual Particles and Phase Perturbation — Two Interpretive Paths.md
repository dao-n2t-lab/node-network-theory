## I. Phenomenon: Interaction Between Two Charged Particles

Two electrons generate an electromagnetic force—they repel each other. In standard physics, this phenomenon requires some mechanism to explain: how does one electron "know" about the existence of the other? What is transmitted between them?

The phenomenon itself is clear: there is a measurable interaction force between two charged particles. But to explain the transmission mechanism of this force, the standard model and the N2T framework offer two completely different paths.

## II. The Standard Model's Interpretive Path: Virtual Particle Exchange

### 2.1 Definition of Virtual Particles

In quantum field theory, the interaction between two charged particles is described by the exchange of "virtual particles":

- Two electrons exchange a "virtual photon," transmitting the electromagnetic force;
- Virtual photons are mathematical terms in the perturbative expansion of quantum field theory, not satisfying the mass-shell relation $E^2 = p^2 c^2 + m^2 c^4$;
- Virtual particles cannot be directly detected; they appear only in calculations.

### 2.2 The Role of Virtual Particles

The core functions of virtual particles in calculations are:

| Function | Role Assumed by Virtual Particles |
|:---|:---|
| Momentum transfer | Virtual particles carry momentum change (push/pull direction) |
| Energy transfer | Virtual particles carry energy change (interaction strength) |
| Phase information | Virtual particles carry spin/polarization information (phase relations between particles) |
| Path summation | Sum over all possible virtual particle exchange paths to obtain the final probability amplitude |

### 2.3 Standard Model Calculation Form (Schematic)

$$M \sim \sum_{\text{all Feynman diagrams}} g^2 \cdot \frac{1}{q^2 - m^2} \cdot \text{polarization factor}$$

where:

- $g$ is the coupling constant;
- $q^2$ is the square of the virtual particle's four-momentum;
- $m$ is the virtual particle mass (if any).

### 2.4 The Ontological Status of Virtual Particles

Virtual particles are an effective computational tool in the standard model. Physicists generally acknowledge that they are not observable physical entities—they are mathematical terms in perturbative expansions, though their mathematical form is structurally similar to that of observable particle propagators. They "work," but do not represent "existence."

## III. The N2T Framework's Interpretive Path: Phase Perturbation and Stress Transmission

### 3.1 Geometric Definition of Interaction

In the N2T framework, the interaction between two node clusters (such as electrons) is defined as:

**Two node clusters establish phase coupling through edges in the node network, with stress transmitted step by step along the edges—no intermediate entity is needed as a "messenger."**

### 3.2 The Mechanism of Interaction

In the N2T framework, the interaction process is:

1. **Phase difference generation**: There is a phase difference between the phases of node cluster A and node cluster B;
2. **Stress propagation along edges**: The phase difference propagates step by step through the edges of the node network, generating phase stress on each edge;
3. **Stress accumulation**: The stress propagates along the path to the target node cluster, changing its phase state;
4. **Conservation of energy and momentum**: Energy and momentum conservation is directly reflected in the direction and magnitude of the stress, without needing to be "carried" and "transmitted" through intermediate particles.

### 3.3 N2T Framework Calculation Form (Schematic)

$$F_{AB} \sim \sum_{\text{paths}} \kappa \cdot \sigma(\Delta \theta, L) \cdot e^{-\lambda L} \cdot \sin(\Delta \theta)$$

where:

- $\Delta \theta$ is the total phase change along the path;
- $L$ is the path length;
- $e^{-\lambda L}$ is the attenuation of the path contribution (the farther the distance, the smaller the contribution);
- $\sin(\Delta \theta)$ is the degree of phase matching (equivalent to the "polarization factor" of virtual particles).

### 3.4 The Position of Virtual Particles in the N2T Framework

In the N2T framework, virtual particles are not necessary. They are a mathematical tool in perturbative expansions; the N2T framework does not introduce them as physical entities. Interactions are directly transmitted through phase differences and stresses, without the need for intermediate entities as "messengers."

## IV. Comparison of the Two Interpretive Paths

### 4.1 Core Differences

| Dimension | Standard Model (Virtual Particles) | N2T Framework (Phase Perturbation) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Interaction transmission mode | Exchange of virtual particles | Phase-difference-driven stress transmission |
| Status of virtual particles | Computational tools, not physical entities | Not introduced |
| Need for intermediate entities | Yes (virtual particles as intermediate states) | No (stress transmitted directly) |

### 4.2 Correspondence of Calculation Formulas

| Standard Model Term | N2T Corresponding Term | Physical Meaning |
|:---|:---|:---|
| $q^2$ transmitted by virtual particles | Phase difference along path $(\Delta \theta)^2$ | Accumulation of stress gradient along the path |
| Coupling constant $g$ | Node coupling strength $\kappa$ | Determined by node density and edge length |
| Virtual particle mass $m$ | Path attenuation constant $\lambda$ | Attenuation of phase perturbation when propagating along edges |
| Propagator $1/(q^2 - m^2)$ | Stress response function $\sigma(\Delta \theta, L)$ | Stress on an edge as a function of phase difference and distance |
| Polarization factor | $\sin(\Delta \theta)$ | Degree of phase matching determines attraction/repulsion |
| Path integral $\sum_{\text{paths}}$ | Sum over all paths $\sum_{\text{paths}}$ | Superposition of stress contributions from all possible paths |

### 4.3 Equivalence of the Two Paths

The computational functions performed by virtual particles in the standard model—transmission of momentum, energy, and phase, as well as path summation—can be equivalently accomplished in the N2T framework through phase differences, edge length deviations, and stress propagation along paths.

Virtual particles are not the only way, nor a necessary way, to describe interactions in physics. The N2T framework provides a fully geometric interpretive path that does not require the introduction of unobservable intermediate states.

## V. Summary

In the N2T framework, virtual particles and phase perturbation represent two different interpretive paths:

- The **standard model** describes interactions through the exchange of virtual particles, which are mathematical tools in perturbative expansions;
- The **N2T framework** describes interactions through the direct transmission of phase differences and stresses along edges, without introducing virtual particles as intermediate entities.

This is not a negation of the standard model, but a different modeling path for the same phenomenon. The challenge for the N2T framework is that it needs to provide quantitative predictive capability with the same precision without introducing virtual particles and their mathematical system—this is one of the core tasks for the framework's future development.

**Virtual particles are computational tools, not physical entities. The N2T framework provides an interpretive path that does not require the introduction of unobservable intermediate states.**
