In the N2T framework, the three states of matter (solid, liquid, gas) are not intrinsic properties of matter, but three network structural states exhibited by the same set of node clusters under different vibration amplitudes and frequencies. The differences between solid, liquid, and gas come from variations in the spatial relationships, connection modes, and stress intensities between node clusters.

## I. Comparison of Node Network Structures of the Three States

We define the differences between the three states using three dimensions: distance between nodes, connection density, and connection strength (stress):

| State | Node Spacing | Connection Density | Connection Strength (Stress) | Macroscopic Manifestation |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Solid | Small (close to minimum stable distance $L_{\min}$) | Very high (fixed connections) | Strong (stable high stress) | Rigid, fixed shape |
| Liquid | Medium (close to natural length $L_0$) | Relatively high (dynamic connections) | Medium (moderate stress) | Flowing, incompressible |
| Gas | Large (much greater than natural length $L_0$) | Extremely low (intermittent weak connections) | Extremely weak (near zero) | Free diffusion |

## II. Solid: Node Clusters Locked in Fixed Lattice Positions

In a solid:

- The average distance between node clusters is close to the minimum stable distance $L_{\min}$；
- The edge connections between node clusters are fixed and ordered (crystal lattice structure);
- The stress on the edges is stable and relatively high, with node clusters locked in fixed relative positions;
- Node clusters can only undergo small vibrations around their equilibrium positions (thermal vibrations);
- If external stress exceeds the edge connection threshold, the lattice structure fractures (shattering, melting).

**N2T mechanism**: vibration amplitude is below the locking threshold $A_{\text{lock}}$，and node clusters cannot脱离 their current connection mode.

**Macroscopic manifestation**: rigid, with a fixed shape, not easily compressible; thermal vibrations manifest as temperature.

## III. Liquid: Node Clusters Continuously "Break-Reform" Connections

In a liquid:

- The average distance between node clusters is close to the natural length $L_0$；
- Node clusters establish stable edge connections, but the connections are dynamic—constantly breaking and reforming, yet always maintained at a high density level;
- The stress on the edges is at a moderate level—sufficient to maintain phase synchronization between adjacent node clusters, but insufficient to prevent relative sliding between node clusters;
- Each node cluster only needs to "turn slightly" to break its connection with its current neighbor and establish a new connection with another adjacent node cluster.

**N2T mechanism**: vibration amplitude is in the range $A_{\text{lock}} < A < A_{\text{escape}}$，connections continuously reconfigure but do not completely脱离 the network.

**Macroscopic manifestation**: flowing, incompressible, with surface tension (the "turning" degrees of freedom of node clusters at the liquid surface are constrained).

## IV. Gas: Node Clusters Almost Completely Detached from the Network

In a gas:

- The average distance between node clusters is much greater than the natural length $L_0$；
- Connections between node clusters are extremely sparse, with most of the time spent in a "freely rotating" state;
- The stress on the edges is extremely weak, nearly zero;
- Node clusters move primarily in straight lines, occasionally undergoing collisions and brief weak connections.

**N2T mechanism**: vibration amplitude exceeds the escape threshold $A_{\text{escape}}$，and node clusters completely脱离 the original network.

**Macroscopic manifestation**: free diffusion, no fixed shape, easily compressible.

## V. The N2T Mechanism of State Transitions

When external conditions (temperature, pressure) change, the vibration amplitude and frequency of node clusters change accordingly, causing the system to cross critical thresholds:

| Phase Transition | Vibration Parameter Change | N2T Mechanism |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Solid $\rightarrow$ Liquid (melting) | Vibration amplitude increases, exceeding $A_{\text{lock}}$ | Node clusters begin to be able to脱离 fixed lattice positions |
| Liquid $\rightarrow$ Gas (evaporation/boiling) | Vibration amplitude further increases, exceeding $A_{\text{escape}}$ | Node clusters completely脱离 the liquid-phase network |
| Gas $\rightarrow$ Liquid (condensation) | Vibration amplitude decreases, falling below $A_{\text{escape}}$ | Node clusters begin to establish stable edge connections |
| Liquid $\rightarrow$ Solid (freezing) | Vibration amplitude decreases, falling below $A_{\text{lock}}$ | Node clusters are locked in fixed lattice positions |

**The essence of temperature**: the statistical average of node cluster vibration amplitude. Temperature increases $\rightarrow$ vibration amplitude increases $\rightarrow$ phase transitions advance toward higher-energy states.

## VI. Why Different Substances Have Different Phase Transition Temperatures

Under the same mechanism, different substances exhibit different phase transition temperatures, due to two underlying parameters:

| Parameter | Effect on Phase Transition Temperature |
|:---|:---|
| Node cluster mass | Heavier node clusters have smaller amplitudes for the same vibrational energy, requiring higher temperatures to reach liquid/gas states |
| Connection strength | Stronger connections require larger amplitudes for node clusters to escape (higher $A_{\text{escape}}$)，requiring higher temperatures for phase transitions |

Therefore, methane, water, and iron have different phase transition temperatures not because "water has different liquid rules," but because their node clusters have different "locking thresholds."

## VII. The Diversity of Compositional Components

The three states do not depend on specific "components":

- Whether it is a methane molecule, a water molecule, or an iron atom, as long as the spatial relationships, connection modes, and stress intensities between node clusters satisfy the conditions for a solid, it macroscopically manifests as a solid;
- As long as the conditions for a liquid are satisfied, it macroscopically manifests as a liquid;
- As long as the conditions for a gas are satisfied, it macroscopically manifests as a gas.

**The three states describe the "network structural state of an assembly of node clusters," not an exclusive property of any "specific molecule."**

## VIII. Summary

In the N2T framework:

**The three states of matter are the network structural states of the same set of node clusters under different vibration parameters:**

- **Solid**: node clusters are locked in fixed lattice positions and can only vibrate around their equilibrium positions;
- **Liquid**: node clusters continuously "break-reform" connections, requiring only a "slight turn" to connect with new neighbors;
- **Gas**: node clusters are completely detached from the network, moving freely with only intermittent weak connections.

Transitions between the three states are driven by vibration amplitude and frequency crossing critical thresholds. Temperature is the statistical average of vibration amplitude, and different substances exhibit different phase transition temperatures because their node cluster masses and connection strengths differ.

**All differences between solids, liquids, and gases can be traced back to changes in the connection modes, connection densities, and stress intensities between node clusters—no additional "phase transition mechanisms" or "molecular forces" need to be introduced as independent assumptions.**
