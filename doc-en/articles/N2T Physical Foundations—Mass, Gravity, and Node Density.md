## I. Mass: The Macroscopic Manifestation of Node Density

In the N2T framework, nodes themselves have no mass. Mass is the statistical average of the density of nodes in space.

### 1.1 Microscopic Definition: Mass Comes from Stress

At the microscopic scale, mass is generated by the stress on edges deviating from their natural length:

- Nodes establish edges between them;
- When the edge length deviates from the natural length $L_0$，geometric stress is generated on the edge;
- This stress is perceived macroscopically as "mass."

**Mass is, at the microscopic level, a stress state, not a property of the nodes themselves.**

### 1.2 Macroscopic Definition: Mass = Node Density

At the macroscopic scale, mass is defined as the number of nodes per unit volume:

$$\rho_m \propto \frac{N_{\text{nodes}}}{V}$$

- Regions with dense nodes $\rightarrow$ large mass $\rightarrow$ produce significant gravitational effects;
- Regions with sparse nodes $\rightarrow$ small mass $\rightarrow$ weak gravitational effects;
- Regions with zero node density $\rightarrow$ no mass.

### 1.3 The Unification of Microscopic and Macroscopic

The microscopic "stress" and the macroscopic "density" are two descriptions of the same phenomenon at different scales:

- In regions with dense nodes, the edges between nodes are more easily compressed or stretched, generating higher stress $\rightarrow$ greater mass;
- In regions with sparse nodes, the stress on edges is lower $\rightarrow$ smaller mass.

**Mass is not a property carried by nodes, but a state of the node network.**

## II. Gravity: The Macroscopic Manifestation of Node Density Gradients

In the N2T framework, gravity is not a "force," but a geometric effect generated when the node network density is non-uniform.

### 2.1 Density Gradients $\rightarrow$ Stress Gradients

When the node density in one region is higher than in surrounding areas, a node density gradient is generated at the boundary between the high-density and low-density regions.

In the node network, the existence of a density gradient means that the edge lengths and phase distributions between nodes are non-uniform $\rightarrow$ that is, a stress gradient is generated:

$$\nabla S \propto -\nabla \rho_{\text{node}}$$

**The essence of the gravitational field is the macroscopic manifestation of node density gradients in space.**

### 2.2 Node Density Distribution Around Massive Celestial Bodies

Around stars, planets, or black holes, the density distribution of the node network is non-uniform:

- Near the celestial body: node density is high (edges compressed, high stress);
- Far from the celestial body: node density is low (edges at natural length, low stress).

This density gradient causes external node clusters to be "pushed" toward the high-density region—this is what we perceive as "gravitational attraction."

### 2.3 Correspondence with General Relativity

In standard physics, gravity is interpreted as "spacetime curvature." In the N2T framework, "spacetime curvature" is the statistical manifestation of node density gradients at macroscopic scales:

| General Relativity Concept | N2T Framework Correspondence |
|:---|:---|
| Spacetime curvature | Non-uniform spatial distribution of node density |
| Geodesic | The path of minimum stress gradient in the node network |
| Gravitational constant $G$ | The proportionality coefficient between node density and stress (determined by network topology) |

**Gravity is not "curved spacetime," but the macroscopic manifestation of node density gradients guiding node clusters to move along the direction of stress gradients.**

## III. The Unification of Mass and Gravity

In the N2T framework, mass and gravity are different facets of the same entity:

$$F_{\text{gravity}} \propto -\nabla \rho_{\text{node}}$$

The magnitude of gravity is determined by the spatial rate of change of node density, not by "mass" itself.
