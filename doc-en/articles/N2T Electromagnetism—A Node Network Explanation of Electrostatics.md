## I. Electric Charge: The Divergence of Phase Stress

In the N2T framework, electric charge is not a property carried by nodes themselves, but the divergence of phase stress in the node network.

A charged particle (such as a proton or electron) is a node cluster. The phase distribution in its shell-layer node network is not uniform—it has a net phase difference. The degree of accumulation of this phase difference in space is the electric charge.

- **Positive charge**: phase stress diverges outward (phase gradient points from center outward);
- **Negative charge**: phase stress converges inward (phase gradient points from outside toward the center).

Mathematically, charge can be expressed as the divergence of the phase stress field $S_\theta$:

$$q \propto \nabla \cdot S_\theta$$

**Nodes themselves do not carry charge. Charge is the macroscopic manifestation of non-uniform phase distribution in the node network.**

## II. The Electric Field: The Spatial Distribution of Phase Gradients

In the N2T framework, the electric field is defined as the distribution of phase gradients in space:

$$\mathbf{E} \propto -\nabla \theta$$

where $\theta$ is the phase distribution of the node network.

When the phase distribution of a charged node cluster generates a phase gradient in the surrounding space, that gradient is the electric field. The electric field strength corresponds to the magnitude of the phase gradient, and the field direction corresponds to the direction of fastest phase decrease.

In a steady state, this phase gradient does not change with time—this is the meaning of "static."

**The electric field is not a "field generated by charges," but the macroscopic manifestation of phase gradients in the node network.**

## III. Generation of Electrostatic Force: Superposition of Phase Gradients

When two charged node clusters approach each other, the phase gradients they each establish in space overlap.

| Charge Type | Phase Gradient Direction | Overlap Effect | Macroscopic Manifestation |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Same sign (positive-positive or negative-negative) | Same direction | Phase gradients conflict, stress increases | Repulsion |
| Opposite sign (positive-negative) | Opposite direction | Phase gradients smoothly connect, stress decreases | Attraction |

The electrostatic force is not a "action at a distance between charges," but rather:

**After the phase gradients of two charged node clusters superimpose in space, the resulting stress difference drives the node clusters to move—toward the direction of lower stress (attraction), or away from the direction of higher stress (repulsion).**

## IV. Correspondence with Coulomb's Law

In standard physics, the electrostatic force between two point charges is:

$$F = k \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}$$

In the N2T framework, the geometric correspondence of this formula is:

| Standard Physical Quantity | N2T Correspondence |
|:---|:---|
| Charge $q$ | Divergence of phase stress $\nabla \cdot S_\theta$ |
| Electric field $\mathbf{E}$ | Phase gradient $-\nabla \theta$ |
| Force $F$ | Stress change from superposition of phase gradients |
| Distance $r$ | Edge path length between two charged node clusters |
| Coulomb constant $k$ | Phase coupling coefficient of the node network |

## V. The Unification of Electrostatics and Magnetism

In the N2T framework, electrostatics and magnetism are two components of the same phase gradient field:

| Phenomenon | Component of Phase Gradient | Characteristic |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Electrostatics | Divergence component of phase gradient (curl-free) | Static, does not change with time |
| Magnetism | Curl component of phase gradient (divergence-free) | Dynamic, related to motion |

- When charges are stationary, the phase gradient contains only the divergence component $\rightarrow$ manifests as an electrostatic field;
- When charges are in motion, the phase gradient generates a curl component $\rightarrow$ manifests as a magnetic field.

**Electricity and magnetism in the N2T framework are two manifestations of the same phase gradient under different conditions—no additional assumptions are needed.**

## VI. Testable Inferences

1. **Electrostatic field in vacuum**: if there were no nodes in external space, phase gradients could not be established, and the electric field would not exist. The fact that electrostatic fields can exist in vacuum indicates that vacuum contains a low-density node network—consistent with the N2T framework;
2. **Electrostatic shielding**: conductive node networks can rearrange their phase distribution to cancel external phase gradients, making the internal phase gradient zero;
3. **Electrostatic induction**: external phase gradients cause the node phases on the conductor surface to redistribute, generating local phase stress accumulation.
