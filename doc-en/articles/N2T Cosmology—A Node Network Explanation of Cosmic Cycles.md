## I. Expansion and Contraction: The Two Phases of the Node Network

In the N2T framework, the expansion and contraction of the universe are not the stretching and compression of "space itself," but changes in the stress distribution state of the node network.

### 1.1 Expansion Half-Cycle: Stress Concentration $\rightarrow$ Edge Lengthening

When the node network is in a "stress concentration" state:

- A large number of nodes are locked into material structures (galaxies, stars, atoms);
- The number of remaining nodes in the vacuum decreases, and the average distance between nodes increases;
- The global average edge length $\bar{L}$ continues to grow—manifesting macroscopically as Hubble expansion.

In the expansion phase, the node network is in a "high-stress locked" state, with remaining nodes in the vacuum being gradually diluted.

### 1.2 Contraction Half-Cycle: Stress Release $\rightarrow$ Edge Shortening

When the node network is in a "stress release" state:

- Some stress in material structures is released (through decay, radiation, structural reorganization);
- Locked nodes return to the vacuum, and the average distance between nodes decreases;
- The global average edge length $\bar{L}$ contracts—manifesting macroscopically as cosmic contraction.

In the contraction phase, the node network is in a "stress release" state, with vacuum node density recovering.

### 1.3 Expansion and Contraction Are Two Directions of the Same Process

In the N2T framework, expansion and contraction are two manifestations of the node network's stress state—not two independent "modes," but two directions of the same stress evolution path:

| Phase | Stress State | Global Average Edge Length Change | Node Distribution State |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Expansion half-cycle | Stress concentration (locked in structures) | $\bar{L}$ increases | Nodes in vacuum diluted |
| Contraction half-cycle | Stress release (structural disintegration) | $\bar{L}$ decreases | Vacuum node density recovers |

## II. The Driving Forces of Stress Concentration and Release

### 2.1 The Driving Force of Stress Concentration: Node Density Fluctuations

In the N2T framework, the driving force of structure formation is the self-amplifying process of node density fluctuations:

- In the early universe, node density was approximately uniform, but small fluctuations existed;
- Regions with higher density generate local stress gradients, attracting neighboring nodes;
- More nodes enter $\rightarrow$ density further increases $\rightarrow$ stress gradients further intensify;
- The cycle continues until nodes are locked into stable material structures.

The formation of stars, galaxies, and galaxy clusters is essentially stress concentration in high-density regions of the node network.

### 2.2 The Driving Force of Stress Release: Phase Unlocking

When material structures reach their stress limit, the internal node phase locking begins to fail:

- Nuclear decay, supernova explosions, and Hawking radiation from black holes are all manifestations of node unlocking;
- Released nodes return to the vacuum, increasing vacuum node density;
- The global average edge length begins to shorten—the contraction phase begins.

**Stress release is not "destruction," but the natural recovery of the node network after stress accumulation exceeds the threshold.**

## III. The Energy Source of the Cosmic Cycle

In the N2T framework, the "energy" of the cosmic cycle is not externally input, but an intrinsic property of the node network's own stress balance.

### 3.1 Energy Comes from Stress, Not from Outside

- In the expansion phase, energy is locked in the stress within material structures;
- In the contraction phase, energy is released back into the node network, driving the redistribution of nodes;
- The sustainability of the cycle comes from the "self-sustaining" nature of the node network—as long as nodes exist, vibration exists, and stress exists.

### 3.2 The Cycle Is Not a "Perpetual Motion Machine"

The total energy of the node network is conserved—it neither increases nor decreases. The transition of the cycle is not "creating energy," but stress cycling between "locked" and "released" states.

### 3.3 The Duration of the Cycle

The cycle length is determined by the following factors:

- **Total number of nodes**: more nodes require longer time for structure formation;
- **Stress accumulation rate**: the amplification speed of node density fluctuations determines the length of the expansion half-cycle;
- **Unlocking threshold**: the maximum stress that node locking can withstand determines when the contraction half-cycle begins.

These factors together determine the characteristic time scale of the cosmic cycle, but their orders of magnitude need to be further determined through numerical simulations.

## V. Testable Inferences

1. **The expansion rate of the universe will slow down and stop**: when the locking of material structures reaches its limit, further stress concentration is inhibited, and expansion terminates;

2. **After expansion terminates, the universe will transition to a contraction phase**: when material structures begin to unlock, vacuum node density recovers, and the global average edge length begins to shorten;

3. **The cosmic cycle has directionality but no "beginning" or "end"**: the universe is cyclical, but the state of each cycle is not identical;

4. **The "age" of the universe is relative**: the duration of each cycle may differ, depending on the initial conditions of the node network in that cycle.
