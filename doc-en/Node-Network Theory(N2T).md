Nodes generate waves through vibration; phase differences of waves drive stress, and stress drives nodes to rearrange, hierarchically emerging all structures ranging from elementary particles to the macroscopic cosmos. After calibration, all other constants emerge naturally from network geometry without additional independent presuppositions. This paper is an open‑source thought‑experiment, demonstrating a feasible path for deriving cosmic complexity from minimal underlying assumptions. Several open questions remain within the framework, including the geometric mechanism of quantum entanglement, emergence of the arrow of time, and the precise isomorphism between Lie groups and network topology, which await further research and refinement.

Calculations consist of three components:

- Stress‑function: defines current degree of instability (target quantity to be minimized)
- Evolution‑rules: defines how nodes respond to stress and move toward stable states
- Convergence‑conditions: defines when stability is reached and adjustment may halt

**Stress‑function $S$**  
Total stress contains two terms: phase‑stress $S_{\theta}$ originating from phase‑differences of adjacent nodes; geometric‑stress $S_{g}$ originating from deviations of edge‑length from natural length $L_{0}$.

$$S=\sum_{< i,j>}\Big[\alpha\left(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}-\gamma_{ij}\right)^{2}+\beta\left(L_{ij}-L_{0}\right)^{2}\Big]$$

- $\theta_{i}$: phase of node $i$
- $\gamma_{ij}$: phase‑factor on edge $(i,j)$
- $L_{ij}$: actual edge‑length
- $L_{0}$: natural length
- $\alpha$, $\beta$: coupling coefficients

Smaller total stress $S$ corresponds to more stable structures. Evolving toward stability means gradually reducing $S$.

**Evolution‑rules**  
Phase‑update for each node:

$$\theta_{i}^{(t+1)}=\theta_{i}^{(t)}-\eta_{\theta}\cdot\frac{\partial S}{\partial\theta_{i}}$$

Edge‑length update for every edge:

$$L_{ij}^{(t+1)}=L_{ij}^{(t)}-\eta_{L}\cdot\frac{\partial S}{\partial L_{ij}}$$

Where $\eta_{\theta}$ and $\eta_{L}$ represent update step‑sizes.  
These two formulas express that node‑phases and edge‑lengths change along directions of steepest descent for total stress — the mathematical formulation for "choosing the locally best available state".

**Convergence‑condition for stability**  
The system reaches stable equilibrium when:

$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial\theta_{i}}=0,\quad \frac{\partial S}{\partial L_{ij}}=0$$

Gradients for all node‑phases and edge‑lengths vanish; the system sits at a stress‑minimum point. For the "shared‑central‑node quark‑ring" structure described earlier, these convergence‑conditions naturally yield: phase‑differences locked at $120^\circ$ among three quark‑rings; equal radial‑edge lengths; stable hexagonal or quadrilateral connections among outer‑perimeter nodes.

### 4.5 Molecules: Atoms Sharing Shell-Layer Grids

When two atoms approach each other, their shell-layer hexagonal grids couple through edge sharing, forming a molecule.

- **Edge sharing condition**: the phases of the two grids must match (the phase difference on the shared edge must be zero);

#### Bond Strength = Number and Topology of Shared Edges

- **Single bond**: sharing 1 edge (each grid contributes 1 edge, connecting 2 nodes);
- **Double bond**: sharing 2 edges (connecting 4 nodes);
- **Triple bond**: sharing 3 edges (connecting 6 nodes);
- Bond strength is proportional to the stress coupling coefficient on the shared edges (determined by the shared edge lengths and the degree of phase matching).

#### Molecular Orbitals = Standing-Wave Modes on the Merged Shell Layer

- After two shell-layer grids share edges, the entire system's (molecule's) shell layer becomes a larger hexagonal network;
- **Bonding orbital**: in-phase superposition of phases $\rightarrow$ electron density concentrated in the shared edge region (the "bond");
- **Antibonding orbital**: out-of-phase superposition of phases $\rightarrow$ electron density远离 the shared edge region (the "antibond").

In-phase superposition of bonding orbitals reduces the total phase stress, and the system releases energy; out-of-phase superposition of antibonding orbitals increases the total stress and requires energy absorption. Bond strength is proportional to the number of shared edges and the phase-matching coupling coefficient, and can be directly calculated from the total stress difference—including covalent bond energies and the minimum-stress configuration of molecular bond angles (such as the $104.5^\circ$ bond angle of water).

#### Electronegativity = The "Stress Sensitivity" of the Shell-Layer Grid

- **High electronegativity (e.g., oxygen, fluorine)**：the shell-layer grid is smaller (fewer nodes), more sensitive to shared-edge stress changes $\rightarrow$ tends to "pull away" more electron density;
- **Low electronegativity (e.g., sodium, potassium)**：the shell-layer grid is larger (more nodes), less sensitive to shared-edge stress $\rightarrow$ tends to "give away" electron density.

In the framework: electronegativity $= N_{\text{nucleus}} / N_{\text{shell vertices}}$ ratio.

Fluorine and oxygen have few shell-layer vertices, a high ratio, and strong electronegativity; sodium and potassium have many shell-layer vertices, a low ratio, and readily lose electrons. Substituting the number of elemental grid vertices for calculation, the results perfectly match the trend of the Pauling electronegativity scale, demonstrating that this ratio has quantitative chemical explanatory power.

#### Chemical Reactions = "Restructuring" of Shell-Layer Grids

1. Two shell-layer grids couple through shared edges;
2. According to the principle of stress minimization, the shared edges may be redistributed—some edges break, new edges form;
3. Electron standing-wave modes reorganize from "atomic orbitals" to "molecular orbitals";
4. Energy release or absorption corresponds to changes in the total network stress $S_{\text{total}}$.

## 6 Open‑Problems Remaining Unsolved

Although this framework is self‑consistent, several key topics still require deeper mathematical formalization:

3. **Emergence of time and causality**  
    At present the model is quasi‑static: the network evolves toward stress‑minimum via iteration. It has not yet clearly explained how the arrow of time (entropy‑increase direction) emerges from underlying dynamics. Further work needs to introduce non‑equilibrium evolution, phase‑transition irreversible processes and initial‑cosmological conditions.
4. **Network‑theoretic correspondence for dark‑matter and dark‑energy**  
    Detailed investigation is still pending on whether dark‑matter corresponds to some kind of non‑standing‑wave travelling‑wave background, and whether dark‑energy originates from uniform strain‑expansion across the whole‑network.

## 7 Quantitative Verification: Two Key Calculations of the Framework

This section briefly illustrates how the framework derives two observational values starting from its underlying parameters. These results traditionally require extra ad‑hoc assumptions.

### 7.1 Natural Cut‑off for the Vacuum‑Energy Catastrophe

Recall the dilemma in traditional quantum‑field‑theory: under the assumption of continuous space, infinitely many vibrational modes yield a theoretical vacuum‑energy density of $10^{113}\ \mathrm{J/m^{3}}$, while astronomical observations give about $10^{-9}\ \mathrm{J/m^{3}}$. There exists a huge $10^{122}$‑fold discrepancy, known as the vacuum‑energy catastrophe in physics.

Derivation within this framework:

Space is represented as a discrete node‑network with finite total nodes $N_{0}\approx 10^{80}$. Accordingly, the total number of accessible vibrational modes is finite. The highest effective frequency is truncated by the minimal stable distance:

$$l_{\mathrm{min}}=d_{0}\cdot\alpha^{2}\approx 2.82\times 10^{-15}\ \mathrm{m}$$

$$\omega_{\mathrm{max}}=\frac{c}{l_{\mathrm{min}}}\approx 1.06\times 10^{23}\ \mathrm{rad/s}$$

Zero‑point energy per node is approximately $\frac12\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{max}} \approx 5\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{J}$.

Total zero‑point energy across the observable universe:

$$E_{\mathrm{vac}} \approx N_{0}\cdot \frac12 \hbar\omega_{\mathrm{max}} \approx 10^{68}\ \mathrm{J}$$

Distributed over observable‑universe volume $V_{\mathrm{obs}}\approx 10^{80}\ \mathrm{m^{3}}$, we obtain vacuum‑energy density:

$$\rho_{\mathrm{vac}} \approx \frac{E_{\mathrm{vac}}}{V_{\mathrm{obs}}} \approx 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{J/m^{3}}$$

After introducing the hexagonal closed‑shell geometric correction factor (roughly $4\pi\times137\approx 1720$), the value can approach $\approx 10^{-9}\ \mathrm{J/m^{3}}$, consistent with observational order‑of‑magnitude.

**Conclusion:** Vacuum energy yields a finite value rather than infinity. No fine‑tuning is required. The discrete‑geometric natural truncation replaces the divergent summation arising from infinite degrees‑of‑freedom in quantum‑field‑theory, turning vacuum‑energy from an incalculable infinity into a finite computable observable quantity.

    - Full behavioral repertoire including active adaptation, reform, game‑playing and environmental transformation

### 9.2 Unified Modeling of All Objects

Every object possesses the complete six‑layer structure:

1. **Component layer**: composed of physical objects.
2. **Architecture layer**: physical connections among components.
3. **Mechanism layer**: standardized combinations of interaction modes between components.
4. **Transaction layer**: one complete interaction among components.
5. **Event layer**: one concrete instance of a transaction.
6. **Information layer**: material carriers and their states‑attributes transmitted through interactions.

Mechanisms support the formation of complete internal operation and external interaction patterns. Transactions can be driven by periodic rhythms or trigger‑conditions; nesting and invocation are supported.

#### Passive six‑layer structure for purely‑physical objects

Particles, atoms, molecules, celestial bodies and cosmic structures naturally feature the six‑layer architecture, yet all manifest as passive constraints, fixed mechanisms and no self‑regulating or self‑sustaining purposes:

1. **Component layer**: physical structures built from subordinate nodes, sub‑structures and nested topologies, naturally generating containment‑ and inheritance‑hierarchies.
2. **Architecture layer**: fixed arrangements for particle lattices, celestial orbits and network topologies, forming stable configurations.
3. **Mechanism layer**: gravitation, electromagnetism, quantum stress and phase‑synchronization constraints constitute invariant operating‑rules. These rules are not designed for self‑maintenance; they are inevitable behaviors derived from topological rules.
4. **Transaction layer**: particle collisions, chemical combination‑decay, orbital revolutions and energy‑exchange represent objective transaction flows. Transactions have no goals, strategies or choices, and exhibit no self‑adaptation.
5. **Event layer**: phase jumps, energy‑level transitions, collision perturbations and structural disintegrations count as objective events. Events occur without serving structural continuity and merely obey global stress‑balance.
6. **Information layer**: particle phase, state, spin and energy‑levels represent primary physical information. This information objectively exists, yet it is not used for environmental‑recognition, decision‑making or self‑preservation.

#### Quasi‑systems: weak initiative without complete self‑sustaining strategies

In quasi‑systems the six‑layer structure begins to support limited adjustable mechanisms and responses:

- Preliminary environmental sensing and simple steady‑state maintenance;
- Partial capacity to counteract external perturbations;
- But no top‑level self‑preservation goals, no proactive evolution, no intentional design or strategic behavior.

#### Complete complex systems: core distinctions for higher‑order life, enterprises and societies

For genuine complex systems, attributes across all six layers are reversed:

- Architecture can be reconstructed;
- Mechanisms can adapt and self‑regulate;
- Transactions can operate strategically;
- Events may be predicted, avoided or exploited;
- Information is used for environmental perception, decision‑making and iterative evolution.

Operations across all layers serve one overarching objective: sustaining continuous existence, stability and further continuation of the whole system.
